How this reads a move

Every player's price has its own daily wobble. Over the 28 captured days, the typical blue-chip moves about 0.4–1% a day and a sub-$200 flier moves about 21% a day — a 28× spread. Ranking the tape by size of move therefore ranks it, in practice, by cheapness. Every move here is instead divided by that player's own normal day, so a 1.3% move by Josh Allen and a 22% move by Ray Davis can sit on the same list and mean the same thing.

"Normal day" is the median absolute deviation of that player's daily percent changes across the captured window, scaled to be comparable to a standard deviation. The median is used rather than the mean because one genuine jump would otherwise inflate the baseline and hide the next real move.

What this does not claim. An unusual move is a statement about this player's price history and nothing else. It is not a reason to trade, and it carries no view on whether the market is right. A move can be unusual because news broke, or because a thin market re-priced on one trade — this surface cannot tell those apart, and it does not try.

Limits worth knowing. 28 days is a short window: a baseline built on it is provisional and firms up as the capture grows. A player's first big move inflates his own baseline for the following month, so a second real move reads smaller than it is. Players whose price sat still for most of the window have no usable baseline and are held out rather than flagged (0 today). Prices come from the market overlay only — the model did not move today, and nothing here is a model output.
