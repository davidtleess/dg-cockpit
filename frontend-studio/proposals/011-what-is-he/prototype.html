What is he, in words you'd actually say?

Every player sits somewhere on his position's ladder. The hobby has names for those places — low-end QB1, high-end WR2 — and this league has its own answer to whether that place is a starting job. Below, our rank and the market's sit on the same ladder: an amber mark where the market puts him, a blue one where we do, and the distance between them is the disagreement. Where our model cannot tell players apart, the blue mark becomes a bar across the ranks it cannot separate.

“Start-grade” counts the players good enough to hold a weekly starting slot in this league's lineup, not the generic top 12. The value axis below is square-root, not linear — on a linear axis about 45% of every position piles into the bottom tenth of the height and the ranks you care about are unreadable. Nobody is filtered out; only the scale changed.

Ranks are over the 337 players both lanes cover. Against the full market board they sit a median of 2 places lower (mean 4.4, max 20). Four of your 27 players are absent entirely — one lane prices them and the other does not, so no comparison exists for them; they are listed at the foot.
How deep each position runs here is the thing to take away, and it is not the same number: startable fades out around QB33, RB39, WR52, TE22. Read those figures, not the bar lengths — each panel's rank axis is scaled to its own pool (69 QB against 153 WR), so equal widths do not mean equal depth. That spread is why fourteen receivers is a thinner asset than it sounds and why one startable tight end is not.
