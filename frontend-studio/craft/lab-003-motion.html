What motion is for on this product

A dynasty roster is a ranked list you re-order to ask a different question — by our rank, by the market's, by who we disagree with most. On this product that re-order happens in one frame: nothing moves, the list is simply different. These are the same twelve receivers from your roster, re-sorted three ways. The list re-renders in its new order. Nothing travels, so nothing connects the old position to the new one. The reader re-finds his player by reading names.

Fade out, re-order, fade back in. It has animation and it costs the same time — but at the midpoint every player is gone, so there is no frame in which the reader can follow anyone.

C · Rows travel

proposed

Each row moves from where it was to where it belongs. The player is on screen in every frame, so he can be followed without being read. Duration comes from the app's own ladder, chosen by distance.

What this is claiming, and what it is not

The claim is object constancy, not polish. Heer & Robertson (InfoVis 2007) tested animated transitions between statistical graphics and found subjects estimated changes more accurately with animation than without — the mechanism being that a mark which travels can be tracked, while one that is re-drawn must be re-identified. Panel B is the control that matters: it is animated, it is smooth, it takes exactly as long as C, and it is worse than A, because a fade breaks the very continuity the animation was supposed to supply. Motion is not the ingredient. Continuity is.

Where it does not apply, on this roster, today. Your QB, RB and TE groups hold three to five players and a re-sort moves a row a single slot. At that distance adjacency does the tracking and the animation is decoration — which is why duration is bound to travel rather than fixed: a one-slot move resolves in 110ms and reads as instant, while the twelve-deep receiver group gets the full 400ms it needs. One rule; the data decides where each transition lands on it.

The alternative that was tested and rejected is the per row, from its own distance option in the control bar. It gives every row the same velocity, which is more physically truthful, and it looks worse: rows arrive at different moments and the list appears to settle in pieces rather than to reconfigure as one object. Try it — the cost is real and visible, and the unified duration is the trade being accepted.

The cost this does carry, stated plainly. Scrub panel C to the midpoint of a full reshuffle and it is busy: nine rows crossing at once, passing through each other, resolving by occlusion. The whole list is not readable mid-flight and this page does not claim it is. What stays readable is the one row you are following, which holds its highlight and its place in front. That is the whole benefit — not a prettier list, but one player who never has to be re-found — and it is worth having only because re-finding him is what panel A costs you every time.

Reduced motion is not "the same thing, faster." Travel is the vestibular trigger, so the substitute cannot be a shorter travel — and it must not be a fade, which is panel B. Switch your system to reduce-motion and this page swaps to the honest substitute: rows are placed instantly, and every row that moved carries a one-off marker of how far and in which direction, so the information the motion was carrying — which things changed places, and by how much — survives the removal of the movement.

The refusal — what must never animate, on any surface

A row's position on screen is ordering. A mark's position inside its track is a value. Moving the first is navigation; tweening the second invents data. This is the same boundary the app's own motion file already states — every intermediate frame a valid data graphic — and it is the one that decides whether motion on this product is craft or a defect.

Every motion value on this page is the product's own. Durations and curves are read from frontend/src/styles/motion.css, a Carbon-derived set the app defines — with a class named .dg-motion-row-settle, commented "row sort/filter settle", that is referenced nowhere in the frontend. Five of its six classes are. Nothing here asks for a new token.

Honest limits. The displacement figures are one roster on one day; a deeper WR group or a shallower one moves the numbers. The 47% of row-transitions clearing the two-row bar is close enough to the 40% threshold that the pooled figure alone would not carry this — what carries it is that the movement concentrates entirely in the one group large enough to lose a row in. And no user study was run here: the panels demonstrate the mechanism Heer & Robertson measured, they do not re-measure the benefit.
