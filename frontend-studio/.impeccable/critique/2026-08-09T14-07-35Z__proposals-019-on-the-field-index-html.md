## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** Domain-authentic in vocabulary and thresholds; category-interchangeable in composition and — more damaging — in its question.

Authored for this product: the axes speak the hobby (YPRR, TPRR, share of his offence's snaps, xFP/game). The reference lines are named, cited, arguable bars, not round numbers ("the 280-touch marker that showed up in 8 of 12 RB1 seasons"). `read_for_catcher` computes "alpha" **relationally** — No.1 target share inside his own offence, not against a flat 25% — which is a dynasty manager's reasoning, not a spreadsheet's. "drafted 1.09" is the hobby's own pick notation.

Interchangeable:
- **The composition is the default analytics shape.** Scatter + two dashed reference lines, above a vertical list of cards with percentile drawers. Relabel the axes and this is a SaaS customer-health board, unchanged.
- **There is no field.** The page is titled "Your roster on the field" and has no lineup concept — no starters/bench, no QB/RB/WR/TE/FLEX/Superflex slots, no taxi, no IR. A dynasty roster has structure; this renders a flat list sorted by routes run.

## Overall Impression

The substance is expert and the shell is a dashboard. This surface argues better than any competitor product — every claim names the bar it rests on and the footer cites where the bar came from — and then presents that argument in a layout that could belong to a sales leaderboard. The single biggest opportunity is not visual: it is that the page answers a 2025 retrospective question when its user is a dynasty manager asking a forward-looking one, and it has no lineup, no age dimension, and no cards for quarterbacks in a Superflex league.

## What's Working

1. **Every claim names its bar, and the footer cites the bar's source.** "past the 280-touch marker that showed up in 8 of 12 RB1 seasons" is falsifiable by the reader. KTC and FantasyCalc hand you a number and no argument; this hands you the argument. For an expert audience that is the difference between a tool and an interlocutor.

2. **A designed architecture of refusal.** NO_TAPE players are named and explicitly not zeroed; a missed week renders as a break in the polyline, never a zero; `slot_rate` and `average_target_distance` are marked "how he is used" and given no ranking, because ranking them would assert that slot receivers are worse. Each is a place a lesser product invents a number.

3. **Relational alpha.** Computing No.1 target share *within his own offence* instead of against a fixed 25% bar. `build.py` shows the work of discovering the flat bar was the wrong test.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Every tooltip renders the literal string `null`
- **What**: `index.html:426` — `tip.append(..., d.thin ? $('div',…) : null)`. The `$` helper filters `null`; `Element.append()` stringifies it. Live: focusing the receivers panel and arrowing right yields `"Austin Hooper · TE / nobody owns him / 1.45 yards per route run · 48% share of his offence's snaps / null"`. Affects 168/171 receiver marks, 79/81 backs, 38/63 QBs.
- **Why it matters**: The tooltip is the only way to name any of 315 otherwise-anonymous dots — the flagship interaction of the flagship graphic. It is also read aloud by the `aria-live` region.
- **Fix**: Spread `...(d.thin ? [$('div',{class:'ow'},'thin sample — the rate has not settled')] : [])` into the append, or guard the append behind `if (d.thin)`.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

- **Why it matters**: He is scanning for the two or three things that changed his outlook. Size, brightness and order all point at whoever played the most snaps — the one thing he already knew.
- **Fix**: Drive card mass from `kind` (already computed); sort each group by `kind`, then by the metric the read is about. Lift his own marks to ≥4.5:1 and drop `open` to ~4:1.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] The percentile bar and the value above it are both percentages and mean different things
- **What**: Odunze — `route participation 91.4%` with the bar filled **85%**; `red zone target share 25%` with the bar at **89%**; `catch rate 48.9%` with the bar at **9%**. Nothing says "percentile." Fill-to-track contrast 2.44:1; track vs `--g3` 1.10:1 (invisible). Every tick value lives in a hover-only `title` — unreachable by touch or keyboard — while the instruction reads "Hover a tick for the value." The two `descriptive` rows draw a track with a median tick and **no marker for the player at all**. Because the three rungs land at a fixed 69/80/90 on every WR metric, the page draws 30 tick marks per player to convey three facts.
- **Why it matters**: Two percentages on one line meaning different things is the fastest way to make an expert distrust a tool. `catch rate 48.9%` with a bar at 9% will be read as "9% catch rate."
- **Fix**: Label the axis once as a percentile axis with 0 and 100 anchored; render his value as a **marker on the axis**, not a fill; draw the three rungs once as gridlines through the whole grid; give descriptive rows an unfilled dot at his percentile.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Mobile destroys the centrepiece
- **What**: At 390px each `.plot` is a **1000px SVG in a 308px scroller** — 31% visible — with no scrollbar, no affordance, no gradient edge. Reference labels truncate ("a weekly starter's role — 14.55 expecte"), player labels clip ("Allen RE"), and the visible window is the **0–25% snap-share range**: the worst quarter of the population, with his best players off-screen right. Page height 18,018px. The only discoverability text says "hover, tap or focus a panel and use ← →" — arrow keys, on a phone.
- **Why it matters**: He opens this with coffee, on his phone. This is the scene the surface was built for and where it fails hardest.
- **Fix**: Below ~700px substitute rather than shrink — one strip per player showing his mark against the league distribution on the y-metric only, or a sorted list with an inline distribution bar.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable adapt`
