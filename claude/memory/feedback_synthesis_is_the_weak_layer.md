**Checking a number and writing the sentence built from it are different reliability classes. The
second is much worse, and the gap does not feel like a gap from the inside.**

2026-09-01. A day-closeout for David was written twice and audited by independent agents twice.
**Both rounds found blocking errors. Every individual figure in both drafts had been verified against
disk; the failures were all in the synthesis.** Specific and repeatable:

- Listed work as awaiting David's word that he had already ruled on and that had already shipped —
  **twice, in two different drafts**, the second time one row below the correction apologising for the
  first.
- **Inverted a clean result into a defect.** A grep returning zero proved an alarm was correctly
  silent; the draft listed it among the day's failures because it made the thesis tidier. The same
  author had reported it correctly hours earlier.
- Transplanted a real warning onto the wrong subject, producing advice that would have trained David
  to dismiss a genuine failure on the first action recommended to him.
- Quoted morning figures in the present tense inside a document asserting everything was re-read.

**The mechanism:** a thesis makes facts feel confirmed. Once "everything today was one defect" existed
as a frame, unrelated findings got bent to fit it, and the bending felt like insight rather than
error. Verification is checked against the world; synthesis is checked against a story.

**How to apply:**
- **Never hand David a summary, closeout, or report unaudited.** His standing practice is to audit
  with independent agents first; it is now 4-for-4 on catching serious errors, and it exists because
  of exactly this.
- **Audit the narrative layer specifically**, not only the figures. Give one auditor the single job of
  arguing the thesis is imposed rather than earned, and of finding shipped work described as pending.
- **State status from the repository at write time, never from your own earlier message.** Most of
  these errors were re-assertions of things that had been true when first written.
- Being right about the parts is not evidence of being right about the whole, and it is the strongest
  feeling that it is.
