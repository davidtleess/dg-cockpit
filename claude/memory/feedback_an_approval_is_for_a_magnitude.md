**2026-09-04, DG-159.** David approved the scale change and both threshold corrections against a
specific picture: his backs falling about ten, quarterbacks and Kraft rising four to five, 18 of
26 changing place, and **one** card changing its verdict (AJ Barner, below replacement to above).
Building it moved the numbers. The truth was backs falling **13–17**, quarterbacks slipping
rather than rising, **21 of 26** changing place, and **four** cards flipping — Kaelon Black going
from comfortably above replacement to below it and down seven places, which he had been told was
a six-place move and nothing more.

The direction was unchanged and the ruling was in hand. Shipping it would have been technically
covered. It went back to him instead, with the corrected magnitudes, and he re-ruled the same way
knowing them.

**Why:** an approval is for a magnitude, not just a direction. *"He already said yes"* is exactly
the sentence that turns a good ruling into an ambush. Two of the three things he had wrong were
things he would have discovered on a Monday morning from his own cards — which is the definition
of a surprise he did not agree to. Greg's ruling on it: treating the signature as void was the
correct reading, and it is a rule worth keeping.

**How to apply:** when the built numbers differ from the numbers a ruling was given against,
re-present them BEFORE it ships, even when the direction is identical and even when the peer
relaying it says "do not re-litigate". Re-presenting a magnitude is not re-litigating a decision —
lead with the correction, state plainly that the direction he chose is unchanged, and name every
card whose SENTENCE changes, not just the ones whose number moves. Sign flips are the ones he
feels; a rank move is not the same event as a verdict flip.

Also learned in the same pass: **watch a guard fire or you do not have a guard.** Three of five
defensive pieces built for this exact morning were wired to a constant the change did not move
and would have done nothing, and the worst of them fired at NONE of four positions on the real
move. All three were found by building a real two-vintage database and running the actual page
code — none by reading it.
